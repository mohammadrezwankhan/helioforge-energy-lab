# Advanced planning and calibration studies

These nine models are registered through the roadmap study contract. They run locally in Python, return finite JSON, and keep synthetic defaults separate from user-supplied data. A successful solver or a small residual is software evidence only; neither is proof of calibration, commercial readiness, survey accuracy, or legal compliance.

## Model details and boundaries

For annual storage, 24-entry tariff vectors repeat by hour of day; 8,760-entry vectors are used directly. The model requires import prices to exceed export prices each hour, which rules out grid arbitrage and makes simultaneous grid import/export strictly dominated. Hourly battery direction binaries are used whenever a negative import/export tariff could reward dissipating energy through a charge/discharge cycle, and also when export is free and discharge wear is zero. With nonnegative tariffs, either positive export value or positive discharge wear strictly penalizes a same-hour lossy battery cycle, so only those cases can use the linear relaxation. Monthly peaks follow the 31/28/31/... day non-leap calendar. A baseline is analytically optimized with the same load, PV, grid limits, export/curtailment treatment, and monthly peak charge. The solver uses `scipy.optimize.milp`/HiGHS with a 0.25–10 second user-set bound and zero relative-gap target; only status 0 with a finite zero gap is accepted. A failed solve does not return an unproven schedule. The seeded synthetic default completed in under five seconds in the targeted local run; this is an observed default-case time, not a general performance guarantee.

Reliability draws one complete weather and generator-state path per independent trial. SOC begins at the supplied state once per trial and follows every hour; it is not reset within the sample. For each trial, annualized LOLE is bounded by the reporting-year hours and EENS by annualized total load. Two-sided Hoeffding intervals use those supports and independent trial outcomes; they remain non-degenerate when no event is observed, at the cost of potentially wide limits. The output reports the supports, event counts, and a low-event warning. Annualization scales a representative horizon; it does not imply that it is measured annual history.

Cointegration uses a rank-one OLS relation normalized on power and an OLS error-correction model for first differences. It reports rank and stationarity tests as not performed. The backtest reserves the final chronological segment for score reporting and emits its exact training and holdout counts. In both models, sample statistical performance is separate from the availability of calibrated, real observations.

The six-zone market model has one reference angle and a linear DC flow equation `f_ij = b_ij(θ_i - θ_j)`. Balance-constraint duals are the model's nodal marginal costs. Losses, reactive power, voltage limits and non-convex generator commitment are excluded.

The finance model reports unlevered project and levered equity flows separately. Contracted generation earns the higher of the calendar-year floor and supplied market price; uncontracted generation earns the supplied market price. Tax losses are carried forward only inside the model; depreciation is straight-line; debt is a level annuity; DSRA is funded from close equity and released once its future scheduled-service target falls to zero; working capital is released at the final operating period. A null IRR means the cash-flow signs are non-conventional or the bounded search found no root.

The hydrogen ledger checks the external electricity input against useful electricity, recovered heat, and all stated conversion, compression, water-treatment, and auxiliary losses. The compressor equation is an ideal-gas reversible isothermal work estimate divided by the stated efficiency; it is not a real-gas or multistage compressor design.
